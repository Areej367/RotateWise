    Learn the science

    NDVI

    A greenness index from MODIS and Landsat. Low values mean bare or stressed fields.

    Evapotranspiration

    Water lost from soil and plants together, highest in hot, dry months.

    Soil moisture anomaly

    SMAP soil moisture compared with normal. Long negative runs signal drought stress.

    Frost risk

    Nights near 0 °C damage young crops and set sowing windows.

    How crops rebuild soil

    🌱 Nitrogen fixers

    Soybean, chickpea and clover feed the next crop and cut fertiliser need.

    🍃 Cover crops

    Fill bare months, reduce erosion and add organic matter.

    🌾 Deep roots

    Break compaction so rain soaks in rather than running off.

    ☀️ Drought-tolerant crops

    Sorghum and millet keep fields productive when water is short.

    Satellite vs ground data

    Satellites give steady coverage for decades but at coarse resolution (SMAP about 9–36 km). Your own soil tests and field notes correct local errors. Combining both is best.

    How scoring works

    Each rotation is rated 0–1 on water use, income, nitrogen fixing, soil cover, organic matter, heat resilience and rainfall fit. Your priority sliders weight these. Growing windows come from the local temperature curve; cover crops fill bare months. Uncertainty rises with the scenario sliders, and every number should be checked locally.

    AI and data-fusion approach

    Implemented: an explainable multi-criteria scoring model plus a robustness test. Each rotation is re-scored in 20 simulated futures (rainfall within 20% either side of your scenario, temperature from 1 °C cooler to 2 °C warmer) and we report how often it stays the top choice. A rotation that wins in most futures is a safer suggestion.

    Data harmonisation: SMAP (about 9 km), GPM (about 10 km), NASA POWER (about 50 km), MODIS NDVI (250 m to 1 km), Landsat (30 m) and ECOSTRESS (70 m) differ in resolution. The planned pipeline aggregates each layer to monthly values on one common grid, resampling fine layers up to the coarse grid and interpolating coarse layers down, then feeds the same features to the model.

    Methods compared: rule-based scoring (used, because farmers can see why), clustering of NDVI time series to find seasonal transitions and bare periods, z-score anomaly detection for soil moisture, and tree-based models for yield proxies when local yield records exist.

    Where local expertise is essential: soil tests and salinity, seed and variety choice, pest history, markets and prices, and water-right or legal limits.

    Accessibility

    Keyboard navigable, skip link, labelled controls, text and symbols alongside colour, light and dark modes, reduced-motion support and printable output.

    Data notice: monthly temperature and rainfall come from NASA POWER long-term climatology (satellite and model based) for one representative point per region.
